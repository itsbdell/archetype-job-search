# Product Strategist — Positioning Brief

> **Note:** This is a fictional example showing how a completed positioning brief looks. The person, companies, and details are all made up. Use this as a reference when building your own.

## Archetype
VP of Product Strategy, Head of Product, Director of Product Management, Chief Product Officer

## Core Positioning
**Product strategist who turns complex technical platforms into category-defining products by combining deep user research with systems-level thinking about where markets are heading.**

## Lead With
1. Took Meridian's developer tools from 3% market share to 18% in two years by repositioning around workflow integration instead of features
2. Built and led a 40-person product org across three product lines at a $2B enterprise SaaS company
3. Created the "Jobs Cluster" framework — now used company-wide to evaluate new product bets against actual user workflows instead of assumed needs
4. Launched Meridian's AI-assisted code review product from concept to $12M ARR in 14 months

## Key Proof Points
- Meridian (Series D, developer tools): Led repositioning of core product line. 3% → 18% market share, $45M → $120M ARR over 2 years. Grew product team from 12 to 40.
- Meridian: Launched AI code review product. 0 → $12M ARR in 14 months. Won "Best Developer Tool" at DevWorld 2025.
- Plinth (Series B, data infrastructure): Designed the pricing model that increased net revenue retention from 108% to 134%. Built the product-led growth motion from scratch.
- Consulting (independent): Advised 6 Series A-C startups on product-market fit, positioning, and go-to-market. Two acquired, one IPO'd.

## Differentiator
Most product leaders come from either a technical track (engineering → PM) or a business track (consulting → PM). Maya comes from user research — she spent 4 years as an ethnographer before moving into product. This means she builds product strategy from observed behavior, not assumed needs, and her teams consistently ship products that users actually adopt rather than products that look good in demos.

## Resume Version
`Maya_Chen_2026_ProductStrategy.md` — emphasizes product outcomes, team building, and market impact over technical depth

## Cover Letter Angles
- "The best product strategy starts with watching people work, not reading market reports" — user research roots → product vision
- Pattern: find the real workflow → reposition the product around it → win the category
- The "Jobs Cluster" framework story — how an internal tool became a company-wide decision-making model
- Contrast: products built for demos vs. products built for daily use — why the latter always wins

## Target Companies
- Developer tools (GitHub, GitLab, JetBrains, Vercel, Supabase)
- Enterprise SaaS at the platform-building stage (Notion, Figma, Canva)
- AI-native product companies (Anthropic, OpenAI, Cohere — product roles, not research)
- B2B companies repositioning from feature-led to platform-led

## Keywords to Match
product strategy, product-led growth, developer experience, platform strategy, user research, product-market fit, go-to-market, pricing strategy, net revenue retention, product organization leadership
